# Example Output: Project Management Tool Onboarding

**Research Question:** Why do users abandon the onboarding flow before completing their first project?

**Researcher's planned question:** "What made it difficult to invite your teammates?"

---

## Council Summary

The research framing mistakenly equates bypassing the setup wizard with abandonment, ignoring that technical evaluators deliberately skip it to test advanced integrations. Drop-off at the invitation step is not premature hesitation — it signals fear of triggering per-seat billing or exposing irreversible setup mistakes to senior staff. Future research must shift from asking why users fail the beginner checklist to investigating how the tool handles technical edge cases, security permissions, and pricing transparency.

---

## Blind Spots Identified

1. Defining 'abandonment' as failing to complete the wizard misses that technical evaluators consider bypassing the basic UI to stress-test integrations a successful product evaluation.
2. The invitation screen is perceived as a high-stakes trigger for per-seat billing — users halt not because inviting feels premature, but because they fear financial commitment before evaluation is complete.
3. Repository connection requirements create hard blockers for evaluators without administrative credentials — a segment the brief did not acknowledge.
4. Assuming technical prerequisites are universally compatible ignores that non-standard architectures and strict contractor access controls actively prevent some users from completing onboarding at all.

---

## Challenged Assumptions

1. The assumption that users drop off because inviting feels 'premature' — users actually halt because the invitation step signals a predatory billing trap or security liability.
2. The assumption that drop-off is caused by the invitation screen itself — in reality, users are often paralysed by anxiety from the previous step or frustrated by broken integrations.
3. The assumption that completing a demo ticket equals successful onboarding — power users view this as useless hand-holding.

---

## Suggested Questions for Your Real User Study

1. Walk me through what goes through your mind when a trial tool asks for code repository permissions.
2. Tell me about a time you evaluated a tool but deliberately avoided inviting your teammates. What drove that decision?
3. Describe a scenario where you made a mistake during a product setup. How did the tool help or hinder you in fixing it?
4. How do you evaluate if a new project management tool can support your specific technical architecture?
5. When testing a new tool, how do you determine what the cost will be once your entire team is onboarded?
6. Tell me how you currently manage project access for part-time contractors without exposing your core codebase.
7. Describe your ideal first 10 minutes of evaluating a tool you haven't decided to buy yet.

---

## Segments to Prioritise

1. **Junior/mid-level tool scouts** — to understand friction for evaluators without repository admin rights or team-wide rollout authority.
2. **Engineering managers and founders** — to uncover what advanced features they need to see in the first 10 minutes for an evaluation to feel successful.
3. **Teams with contractors or non-standard architectures** — to test where rigid integration requirements break the onboarding flow entirely.

Finding: Say-do gap is predicted by emotional complexity, not expertise level.
